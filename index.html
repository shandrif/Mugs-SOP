How it works

Three checks, one daily file.

01

Direct violation

One invoice has more than two mugs of one colour, for example 6 × Al-Haram Beige.

02

Split-invoice run

The same colour rung on back-to-back invoices, each within the limit, adding up to more. Scored against the branch’s normal pace, so a busy Haram queue isn’t treated like a 1 AM burst.

03

Zero-quantity lines

Lines with 0 quantity or SAR 0, likely voids or returns, listed for review.

Method

How the checks work

1. Rebuilding invoices

The export has no invoice number, so lines with the same branch + closing time (to the millisecond) count as one invoice. If a future export includes an invoice or receipt column, the app uses it automatically.

2. Direct violations

Any invoice with more than the limit of one item (or colour, depending on the setting).

3. Split-invoice runs

For each branch and colour, invoices are put in time order. Invoices no more than the max gap apart are chained into a run. A run is flagged when it has at least the minimum number of invoices, every invoice is within the limit, and the run total is over the limit.

4. Risk score (0–100)

  • Invoices in run: up to 30 pts (5 per extra invoice)
  • Mugs over the limit: up to 25 pts (2.5 per mug)
  • Burst vs normal pace: up to 30 pts. How many times faster the branch sold this colour during the run than over the rest of the day.
  • Identical baskets: up to 15 pts. Share of invoices in the run with exactly the same contents (runs of 3+).

Limits of this version

Without a card token, customer mobile or cashier ID in the export, a split run is likely, not proven. A real queue of different customers can look the same. Check High runs with the branch, POS logs and CCTV.
